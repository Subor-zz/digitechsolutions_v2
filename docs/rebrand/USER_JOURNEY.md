# Rebrand Prototype User Journey

## Primary visitor

An operations or business leader in a Dutch SME or scale-up who feels recurring delay, rework, and dependency across inboxes, spreadsheets, handoffs, and aging software, but does not yet know whether the root problem is process or application architecture.

## Secondary visitor

An IT or application owner evaluating whether Digitech understands dependencies, security boundaries, continuity, phased change, and handover well enough to be a credible modernization partner.

## Trigger

- Repeated manual handoffs or duplicate entry.
- Exceptions are discovered late.
- A critical internal application is difficult to change.
- Knowledge sits with one person or supplier.
- Growth exposes process and system limitations.
- A team suspects modernization is needed but cannot safely choose automation, integration, refactoring, or rebuilding yet.

## Awareness level

The visitor is problem-aware and risk-aware, but often solution-uncertain. Some arrive looking for implementation capacity; the page must reframe the first decision from “which tool or developer?” to “what is actually causing the drag, and what is the smallest responsible intervention?”

## Pain points

- Waiting between people and systems.
- Rework and inconsistent information.
- Missing audit trail and unclear ownership.
- Fragile integrations and unsupported dependencies.
- Fear that a modernization project becomes a disruptive full rewrite.
- Limited internal time to diagnose and coordinate the work.

## Objections

- “You will automate away the exceptions that make our process work.”
- “This will become a costly rewrite.”
- “A scan is paid presales.”
- “AI-assisted work means black-box delivery.”
- “A founder-led studio may become another single-person dependency.”
- “We cannot share sensitive system data through a website.”
- “We do not know which route to select.”

## Information needs

1. A concrete description of the operational pattern.
2. A clear explanation of why process and software must be considered together.
3. Evidence that Digitech chooses among multiple interventions rather than defaulting to automation or rebuilding.
4. A visible distinction between workflow-led, application-led, and combined situations.
5. A bounded method with review, risk, and handover gates.
6. Honest proof standards and explicit limits.
7. A tangible description of the Modernization Scan output.
8. A low-risk way to continue without already knowing the solution.

## Trust requirements

- Direct accountability through Subor, with no implied large internal team.
- Concrete artifacts and decision logic instead of generic quality badges.
- No unsupported metrics, logos, certifications, testimonials, or availability promises.
- Human decisions and exceptions remain visible in process illustrations.
- Clear non-fit and security boundaries.
- A scan result that remains useful without a follow-on build.
- Privacy-safe next steps that do not request credentials, source code, or sensitive records.

## Conversion path

`Recognition -> Understanding -> Trust -> Relevance -> Evidence -> Low-risk next step`

### Recognition

The hero names operational drag and shows fragments from both workflow and application contexts. The visitor should think: “this describes the space between our tools and teams.”

### Understanding

The friction-to-flow sequence shows how handoffs, duplicate entry, and legacy constraints interact. The diagnosis section explains that process, data, systems, risk, and ownership are assessed before a solution is chosen.

### Trust

The decision lens and controlled method show explicit alternatives, quality gates, and transfer. Founder-led copy clarifies who is accountable and where specialist input may be added.

### Relevance

Three route choices let visitors recognize workflow-led, application-led, or combined/unsure situations without requiring a technical diagnosis.

### Evidence

Because approved cases are absent, the page exposes the evidence standard itself: classification, observed state, decision, delivered scope, result context, limitations, and what was deliberately not built.

### Low-risk next step

The final scan section states the confirmed outputs: current-state map, friction and risk register, dependency map, recommended route, and phased first step. The CTA moves to a prototype contact panel, not a fake submission flow.

## Section-by-section journey map

| Section | Visitor question | Objective | Exit or next action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Header and hero | “Do you understand what is slowing us down?” | Recognition, category, founder-led signal, primary action | Scan output or method |
| Hidden drag | “Where is the real cost or risk?” | Make handoffs and dependencies concrete | Workflow route |
| Diagnosis | “Will you prescribe automation or a rewrite?” | Establish judgment and multiple intervention options | Scan explanation |
| Expertise routes | “Which situation resembles ours?” | Self-selection without irreversible diagnosis | Workflow, application, or combined path |
| Controlled method | “How do speed and quality stay visible?” | Explain decisions, gates, artifacts, and transfer | Review method or continue |
| Evidence standard | “What can I actually verify?” | Replace empty social proof with transparent proof criteria | Future case expectations |
| Founder-led | “Who is accountable, and what are the limits?” | Resolve team-size and dependency concerns | Continue to scan |
| Scan close | “What do I receive before committing to a build?” | Make the first step tangible and proportionate | Contact or email fallback |

## Primary CTA

`Start met de Modernization Scan`

In this prototype it anchors to the scan explanation and then to a non-submitting contact option. In production it should lead to the approved scan page before intake.

## Secondary CTA

`Bekijk de werkwijze`

This supports skeptical evaluators who need control and transfer detail before considering contact.

## Drop-off risks

- The visitor still cannot tell workflow from application modernization.
- The scan sounds like an open-ended audit or disguised sales call.
- Missing price and duration make the offer feel unfinished.
- Technical language becomes more prominent than business recognition.
- Founder-led is interpreted as insufficient capacity or a new key-person dependency.
- Motion obscures content or makes the page feel experimental rather than controlled.
- The absence of approved cases makes the page feel unproven.
- A contact interaction appears to collect data without a real privacy and processing setup.

## Prototype success criteria

- The first viewport communicates modernization, both workflow and application scope, founder-led accountability, and the scan CTA.
- The uncertain visitor can select the combined path without feeling they chose incorrectly.
- Every section advances one question in the conversion path.
- All information remains available without motion or client-side JavaScript.
- No illustrative object can be mistaken for a client case or measured result.
